Distributed databases rely on write-ahead logs to maintain data durability across node failures. When a client issues a write request, the leader node appends the transaction to its local log before sending replication RPCs to follower nodes. Each follower writes the entry to disk and acknowledges receipt. Once a majority of followers acknowledge the write, the leader commits the transaction and applies it to the state machine.

This consensus protocol guarantees consistency even if individual network partitions occur. Engineers can configure snapshotting intervals to prevent logs from growing indefinitely. Background compactions purge obsolete segments without interrupting ongoing read traffic. By decoupling transaction logging from disk flushes, systems achieve high throughput while maintaining deterministic crash recovery guarantees across all operating conditions.
