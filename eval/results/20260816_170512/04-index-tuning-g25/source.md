High-volume event logging tables in PostgreSQL demand careful index selection to maintain performance. Choosing the right index type is crucial for efficiently querying millions or billions of records.

**B-Tree Indexes:** The default and most versatile index type, B-Tree is excellent for single-column indexing. Use it for primary keys (`id`), foreign keys, and columns frequently used in `WHERE` clauses for equality or range comparisons, like `timestamp` (e.g., `WHERE timestamp BETWEEN '...' AND '...'`) or `event_type`. B-Trees are highly effective for point lookups and ordered scans, making them a go-to for most standard queries on your log data.

**BRIN Indexes (Block Range INdex):** Ideal for very large tables where data is naturally correlated with its physical storage order, such as `timestamp` columns in append-only event logs. BRIN indexes store summaries of data ranges for physical blocks, making them extremely small and efficient. They excel at range queries (`WHERE timestamp > '...'`) by quickly eliminating large portions of the table, especially when the query range aligns with the data's chronological order, leading to significant I/O savings.

**GIN Indexes (Generalized Inverted iNdex):** When your event logs include complex data types like arrays (e.g., `tags` or `categories`), JSONB (`metadata`), or require full-text search, GIN indexes are indispensable. GIN indexes store an inverted list of values, allowing fast lookups for elements *within* these complex types. For instance, `WHERE tags @> ARRAY['error']` or `WHERE metadata @> '{"user_id": 123}'` greatly benefit from a GIN index by efficiently locating rows containing specific elements within the structured data.

Selecting the appropriate index involves understanding your query patterns and data characteristics. Often, a combination of these index types will provide optimal performance across diverse workloads. Always test your index choices with realistic data volumes and query loads.
